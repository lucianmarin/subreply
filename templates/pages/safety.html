Subreply is the only truly safe social network on the internet. By stripping away media uploads, file sharing, algorithmic amplification and dark patterns, Subreply eliminates entire categories of online harm at the architectural level before they can ever exist.

Published child safety standards

These standards apply to subreply.com, the Subreply API and every official Subreply client, including the Subreply Android app (com.lucianmarin.sub). They describe how Subreply prevents, detects, reports and removes Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE) and Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM). This page is our published Child Safety Standards declaration and is kept current. Last updated: November 18, 2026.

Zero tolerance for CSAE and CSAM

Subreply enforces a strict, zero-tolerance policy against Child Sexual Abuse and Exploitation (CSAE) and Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM). Because Subreply does not support image, video or file uploads of any kind, visual CSAM cannot be hosted or shared on our servers.

We strictly prohibit using Subreply or any third-party Subreply client to endanger, groom, sextort, traffic or otherwise exploit minors through text, external links or private messages. The following conduct is prohibited without exception:

  • Uploading, posting, requesting, linking to or distributing CSAM in any form.
  • Grooming, sextortion, sexual extortion, trafficking or sexualization of minors.
  • Using posts, replies, profiles, mentions, private messages or external links to contact, lure or exploit a minor.
  • Creating accounts, or adapting clients, to evade enforcement of these rules.

Any attempt to share links to CSAM, solicit minors or engage in predatory behavior results in immediate and permanent account destruction without prior warning.

Safe by design

Unlike traditional social networks that struggle to moderate millions of uploaded photos and videos after the fact, Subreply is strictly text-only. Our deliberate constraints make Subreply uniquely immune to visual abuse and exploitation:

  • Text-only architecture — no images, videos, audio or file attachments can ever be uploaded, stored or transmitted on Subreply.
  • Strict character limits — posts and messages are limited to ASCII characters, standard emojis and a maximum of 640 characters.
  • Intentional linking — at most one external link, mention or hashtag is allowed per post, preventing spam and coordinated abuse.
  • No algorithmic rabbit holes — feeds are strictly reverse-chronological with zero viral loops, recommendations or engagement bait.
  • Age requirement — Subreply requires members to be at least 16 years old to register and participate.

Reporting a child safety concern

Anyone can report child safety concerns, abusive behavior or policy violations directly within the app or on the web without leaving Subreply:

  • Email our Child Safety Point of Contact at support@subreply.com. This inbox is monitored and is the fastest way to reach us about CSAE, CSAM or the safety of a minor.
  • Send a direct message in-app to @lucian or @subreply to report any account, post or link.
  • Delete unwanted replies to your own posts before responding to them, or unsend and clear private messages at any time.
  • For emergencies, contact your local law enforcement. In the United States you can also report to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC).

Reports involving the immediate risk of harm to a minor are prioritized. We aim to acknowledge child safety reports within one business day.

Child Safety Point of Contact

Subreply maintains a designated Child Safety Point of Contact who is prepared to discuss our CSAM prevention practices and our compliance with child safety policies:

  • Role — Child Safety Point of Contact, Subreply
  • Email — support@subreply.com
  • In-app — direct message to @lucian or @subreply

The contact details above are kept current in our published standards and in our app store child safety declaration.

Legal compliance and enforcement

Subreply complies with all applicable child safety laws and regulations. Upon obtaining actual knowledge of any CSAE activity or external links to CSAM, we immediately remove the offending content, permanently destroy the associated accounts and report confirmed violations to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) and relevant law enforcement authorities.

  • We preserve and, where legally required, disclose relevant records to law enforcement.
  • We remove confirmed CSAE content and terminate the associated accounts without prior warning.
  • We cooperate with NCMEC, regional child safety hotlines and law enforcement investigations.
  • Enforcement decisions can be appealed by emailing support@subreply.com.
